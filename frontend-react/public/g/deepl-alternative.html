What you get back

  • The deliverable is the original file with the translation written back into it — tables, headers, footnotes and columns stay where they were.
  • A glossary is a gate, not a suggestion: required wording is enforced before delivery and the same term never comes out two ways.
  • Cost is points-based and estimated before the job runs, with the same formula billing uses.

Why this is different

DeepL writes fluent prose — for a single paragraph of general foreign text it is enough, and we do not claim to beat it on that. The difference shows up on a document: what you download, and whether your terminology survived the trip.

Cost is published, not hidden: the estimator on our compare page uses the same formula as billing, and you see the estimate before you commit.

Questions

What does an alternative need to do that a text translator does not?

Take the file apart and put it back together. Translating a DOCX into a text box is the easy half; returning a DOCX is the part that saves the afternoon.

Can I bring my own terminology?

Yes — upload a glossary or termbase as a knowledge package. Entries are applied before delivery, so a term is decided once instead of per paragraph.

Is there something to compare before committing?

Translate a sample document. Layout write-back and terminology either show up in the downloaded file or they do not; you do not have to take a word for it.

What does it cost?

Points, estimated up front. The public estimator uses the same formula as billing, so the number you see before the job is the number that gets charged.
