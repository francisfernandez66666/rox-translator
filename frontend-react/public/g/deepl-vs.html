DeepL vs LangCross — decided by what you download, not by one sentence

Comparing sentence quality is the least useful thing you can do with two tools that deliver different artifacts. Here is the split we would use.

Run one of your own documents — no signup to try

Upload the file, download the same file translated.

What you get back

  • Delivery: translated text versus your original document rewritten in place — styles, tables and footnotes included.
  • Control: glossary as an enforced gate before delivery, plus per-department budgets, SSO and an audit trail on the same account.
  • Money: points per job on our side, estimated before the job runs, with the formula published on the compare page.

Why this is different

DeepL is strong at prose and we will not pretend a single general sentence is where this contest is won. The honest comparison is scope: one tool returns translated text, the other returns your file with the text in it.

Cost is published, not hidden: the estimator on our compare page uses the same formula as billing, and you see the estimate before you commit.

Questions

Which one is better for documents?

Ask a different question: which one gives you back a document. That is the branch this comparison actually turns on.

Is the difference visible in a test?

Yes, in about two minutes: upload a table-heavy page to both and open the downloads side by side. Nothing here requires believing a claim.

Do you handle terminology differently?

Terminology is applied before delivery as a constraint, not offered as a post-edit suggestion, so a term does not drift between paragraphs.

Should a team pay for both?

Some do. If most of your work is a paragraph you are reading, not shipping, a general translator is the cheaper answer; if most of it is a file, it is not.
